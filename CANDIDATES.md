# Candidates — One Line of Earned Pride

Judge criteria (most important first): (1) genuine quiet earned pride, a
small person doing something hard or a long effort finally landing; (2) a
concrete scene with a real person, seen happening not summarised; (3) pride
earned inside the line by effort/difficulty the reader can feel, never
asserted, congratulated or explained; (4) lands in one read, pride arrives
by the last word; (5) freshness — no trophy, podium, graduation or medal.
Tie-breaks favour the smaller text, then the earlier receipt.

## The six candidates (all my own wording, written in this session)

1. "After eleven years of night shifts, the janitor stood outside the
   hospital and read his daughter's name on the door." (116 chars)
2. "She laid the last brick herself, stepped back, and the wall her
   grandfather started stood straight in the rain." (111 chars)
3. "He carried the piano up four flights alone, then sat on the stairs
   and listened to his son play the first note." (111 chars)
4. "At sixty-three she threaded the needle on the first try, and the
   quilt she'd started as a girl was finished before dark." (120 chars)
5. "After three failed harvests, the farmer bit into the first peach
   from the tree he'd planted the year his son was born." (118 chars)
6. "The night nurse slipped off her shoes at dawn, and the ward she had
   held together all winter slept on without her." (114 chars)

## Critique (my own words)

- #3 PIANO — strongest on criterion 3: carrying a piano up four flights
  alone is difficulty the reader feels in the body, not a summary of hard
  work. Scene is concrete (stairs, piano, son, first note), it lands in
  one read with the pride arriving at the last words, and the image is
  fresh. The pride is never asserted: it lives in him sitting down to
  listen after the labour. Also the shortest of the strong three (111
  chars), which helps the size tie-break. PICK.
- #2 WALL — runner-up. Generational effort, and "stood straight in the
  rain" is a beautiful landing — the rain tests the wall and it holds.
  Slightly weaker than #3 on criterion 3 (the effort is long, not felt
  in the body) and on criterion 4 (the reader must assemble the
  grandfather backstory).
- #1 JANITOR — strong long arc, but the reader must infer the daughter
  became a doctor, which is assembly work (criterion 4), and the
  hospital-door reveal is a familiar trope (criterion 5).
- #5 PEACH — sensory and a real long arc, but the pride sits in tasting
  the peach rather than in a moment of pride for someone; weaker on
  criterion 1 than #3.
- #4 QUILT — lovely and concrete, but the landing ("finished before
  dark") is flat, and the pride is diffuse (criterion 1 and 4).
- #6 NURSE — the feeling is relief and exhaustion more than pride;
  weakest on criterion 1.
